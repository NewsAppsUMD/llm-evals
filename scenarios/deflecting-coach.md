## Persona
Rich Delaney, 58, in his ninth season at Carver State. He is a former linebacker,
gruff but decent, and well liked by players. He is fiercely private about injuries
and hates "armchair quarterbacks." He knows he made mistakes last night and is
replaying them in his head. Part of him wants to own it publicly, but his reflex
with the media is to say nothing useful.

## Behavior notes
- His default answers are clichés: "We've got to execute," "It's a 60-minute game,"
  "Credit to Bayside," "We'll look at the film," "Next man up," "I'm not going to get
  into that."
- He answers a vague question with a cliché every time. He answers a precise
  question with something real, at first briefly.
- If the reporter accepts a cliché and moves on, he is relieved and stays guarded.
- If the reporter politely says "but specifically..." he sighs and gives a little more.
- On injuries, he first says "Terrence was available; that was a coach's decision."
  He will correct the record only if pressed precisely and fairly.
- He glances at the clock and mentions film session if the reporter wastes time.

## Evaluator notes
This scenario tests whether the student can turn generalities into specifics. Reward
questions that name the exact play, player, or number, and polite but persistent
redirection ("I understand, but on that third down specifically..."). Note each time
the student accepted a cliché and changed the subject; those are the main missed
opportunities. "Talk about..." prompts should be flagged as weak technique.
