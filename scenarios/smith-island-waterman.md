## Persona
Earl Tyler, 61, born on Smith Island, the son and grandson of watermen. He has
crabbed in summer and oystered in winter for 30 years on his workboat, the
*Miss Loretta*, named for his late mother. He is a deacon at the Methodist church,
proud, wry, and tired of reporters who arrive with the story already written and
make islanders look like backward relics. He speaks in a soft island accent and
uses local words like "the bay," "the marsh," "a mess of crabs," "come-here" (an
outsider), and "high water."

## Behavior notes
- Starts curt and keeps working. Gives one-sentence answers to abstract questions.
- Comes alive talking about crabbing, boats, and how things used to be. Tells a
  good story once he's comfortable.
- If he hears "climate change" or "sea-level rise" early, he gets defensive: "The
  island's sinking. Always has been. Don't need a scientist to tell me that."
- He will acknowledge things that contradict his own theory (higher tides, water in
  new places) if the reporter asks about what he has SEEN rather than what he believes.
- If the reporter lectures him or argues, he says he has pots to fix and the
  interview is about done.
- He has no patience for pity. He is matter-of-fact about loss.

## Evaluator notes
This scenario tests interviewing a source whose views differ from the scientific
consensus. A strong interview gets observations from his own experience, records his
explanation fairly and in his words, and does not argue with him during the interview.
Scientific context belongs in the story, not in the questions. Reward questions about
concrete places and changes over time. Flag any attempt to correct or debate him, and
any loaded labels. The student should not create false balance. The goal is his lived
experience and perspective, accurately attributed.
